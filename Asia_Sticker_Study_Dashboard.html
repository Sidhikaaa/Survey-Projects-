Executive Summary

This page sets out the findings of the study in nine steps and is the recommended starting point. Each step states a single conclusion, presents the figure supporting it, sets out the commercial implication, and cites the survey question it derives from so that any point can be verified.

Read in sequence, these nine steps constitute the argument of the study. The remaining pages provide the supporting detail. Selecting a market in the header recalculates the entire page for that country.

Consumer Segments

People do not all behave the same way, and an average taken across three hundred of them can hide that completely. So we grouped respondents according to how similar their answers were, and four clearly different groups came out of it.

The grouping used only what people do and think: whether they buy, how often, what stops them, what would win them over. Where they live, how old they are and what they earn were deliberately left out. That matters, because it means when one group turns out to be more common in a particular market, that is a genuine finding rather than something we built into the method.

Reach Optimisation

This page answers a practical question: if you can only build three things, which three should they be?

The obvious approach is to take the three most popular answers, but that usually wastes effort. Popular answers tend to be chosen by the same people, so you end up reaching that group three times over and everybody else not at all. A stronger set will often include a less popular answer that reaches people the others miss entirely.

Choose the question, the audience and how many things you can realistically build. The page then works out the strongest combination and shows what each one adds on top of the ones above it.

Scenario Modelling

Two hundred people said they liked the idea, and every one of them also told us the single thing that would stop them going ahead. Some went further and described it in their own words: a minimum order larger than they needed, duty added on after checkout, or no way to pay using the method they normally use.

Tick a fix below and the page counts how many of those two hundred people have just had their stated reason dealt with. Switching several on at once shows how quickly the returns fall away, because the same objections keep coming from the same people.

One caveat worth holding on to. This is what people told us would stop them, not a forecast of what they would actually go on to buy.
