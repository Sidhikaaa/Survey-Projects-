Reading The Segment Filter

The segments do not group carpenters by who they are. It groups them by how they behave. Respondents were clustered on how similar their answers were across four things only: how firm their intent was leaving for the shop, what happened to that intent at the counter, how they react to a price increase, and what they say decides their choice.

Market, age and years of experience were deliberately held out of the grouping. That is why a segment turning out to be more common in one market is a finding you can act on, rather than something the method put there. Click any of the four in a chart to filter the whole dashboard.

Counter Conversion Loss

The number the study exists to produce. An unknown share of formed preference is converted away at the counter, and that loss is invisible in secondary sales because the sale still happens. Here it is sized, decomposed into stock-out substitution, active retailer push and default purchases where no intent existed, and then read for depth rather than incidence alone.
